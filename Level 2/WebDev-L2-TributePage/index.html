Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam

Scientist, Teacher and the People's President

Remembering a visionary who inspired millions of young minds.

Biography

Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. He developed a strong interest in science and learning from an early age. He later specialized in Aeronautical Engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology.

Kalam made important contributions to India's space programme while working with the Indian Space Research Organisation. As Project Director of the SLV-III programme, he played a major role in the successful launch of the Rohini satellite in 1980.

He later worked with the Defence Research and Development Organisation and contributed to India's guided missile development programme. His scientific achievements earned him the popular title "Missile Man of India."

In 2002, Dr. Kalam became the 11th President of India. After his presidency, he continued teaching, writing and interacting with students. His dedication to education and young people made him an inspirational figure across India.

His Legacy

Dr. Kalam is remembered for combining scientific achievement with a strong commitment to education and national development. His books, speeches and interactions with students continue to encourage young people to learn, imagine and work toward a better future.
